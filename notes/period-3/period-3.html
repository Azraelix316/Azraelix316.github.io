John Locke

John Locke was one of the key leaders of the Enlightenment, spreading many ideas notably the idea of a Social Contract and Natural Rights, and contributed a lot to the early Enlightenment and created much of the novel ideas around government and government control.
John Locke is important for being a trailblazing philosopher who developed a lot of ideas on governments and society, and led to the rise of more Enlightenment thinkers.

John Locke

Tabula Rasa

One of John Locke's ideas, that man is a blank slate without any innate knowledge and preconceptions. This challenged religious legitimization, stating that no man was born with the right to rule over anyone else.
The belief in Tabula Rasa was a stepping stone to other ideas in fields of psychology, sociology, and politics, and helped shape ideas on education and nature vs. nurture. All of these ideas weakened ideas behind birthright, which weakened kings and made it easier to rebel.

Tabula Rasa

Social Contract

The social contract significantly influenced other revolutions and revolutionary rhetoric. It was a philosophical idea that individuals consent to rule, and that they can remove their consent at any time.
This groundbreaking idea encouraged revolutions, and delegitimized governments that solely relied on religious hierarchy. Thus, rulers should not use religion and instead use their actions and policies as justification for ruling their population.
Due to higher literacy rates and the printing press, revolutions such as the American Revolution or the French revolution referenced this idea when pushing the issue of independence, stating that their rulers had infringed upon their rights.

Social Contract

Montesquieu

Montesquieu was a French philosopher who developed the system of government of checks and balances, which was used in the United States's revolution and helped lay the groundwork for current democratic systems.
His ideas surrounding checks and balances and the seperation of powers influenced modern democratic governments heavily, and helped many states see the potential of having a more equal form of government with a weaker monarchy, motivating revolutions and helping revolutions gain a stable political structure.

While Napoleon enacted some beneficial laws to turn France into a meritocracy, the social structure remained relatively similar with only increased social mobility in the military and bureaucracy.

Nationalism

Nationalism, strong belief of a public identity, was increased due to shared languages and standardized languages, leading to political unification of previously independent states in Italy and Germany (Bismarck and Count of Cavour). 
Other states such as Japan and India also developed elements of nationalism, such as Japan, which resisted European colonialism, and India was in a similar situation creating the Indian National Congress in 1855 in order to resist British rule. 
These all stemmed from a similar idea of a similar group of people having a strong political/national identity, much larger than any loyalty seen before. This is significant because it allows for deeper connections and helped nations develop and maintain power by making massive populations easy to control.
However, in some nations like France or America it stems from civic nationalism, where it’s the government and the citizenship of being “American” that really leads to a nationalistic view.

Most states would heavily utilize Enlightenment Rhetoric in order to encourage revolutions, with many of the founding documents utilizing Enlightenment rhetoric to emphasize and prove their ideas of independence.
America's Declaration of Independence (Life, Liberty, Happiness = natural rights, social contract) and Constitution (3 branches) are both filled with references to Enlightenment ideals, and Common Sense was filled with Enlightenment ideals as well.

Capitalism

Adam Smith argued for supply and demand to control the market and to leave it alone (laissez faire), instead of using mercantilism which argued for strict government control.
This suggestion allowed nations to reconsider their economic theories and this led to some colonies like the U.S becoming angry with strict government regulations that would end up being inferior under this new economic governing style, leading to revolutions.
This led to a belief of more free-market economics and reduced interference by governments, leading to enterprises with poor working conditions and low regulation and worker protection.
Capitalism ended up being a huge change, replacing mercantilism as the dominant economic system. The promotion of free trade and competition led to the rise of more companies and increased amounts of trade, allowing some to get extremely wealthy.

Capitalism

Regulations

Due to poor working conditions, there would be multiple waves of reform in order to push for improved living conditions in these new industrialized countries.
The 1st wave of reform focused on fixes to severe injustices, such as child labor, sanitation, etc. while the second wave focused on social reforms and public education to prevent the Marxist revolution and to limit public unrest.
Public education and the right to vote were also expanded upon in order to prevent the threat of a revolt.
Reform efforts were assisted by the threat of Marxist revolution, which was a serious threat for most nations who wanted to keep their power - demonstrated in the Russian Revolution (not this period though).

Regulations

Inequality

With the rise of Capitalism and increased economic expansion, some social classes gained more than others. The lower classes stayed relatively the same, trading in agricultural jobs for industrial ones, and in some sectors worsened - for example, families drifted apart as child laborers worked in different factories from their parents.
However, for the middle class and upper classes, they would benefit greatly from the industrial revolution, increasing in wealth dramatically. They would treat lower classes poorly however, employing Social Darwinism to justify their higher status compared to the lower working class.

Imperialism

Imperialism in this period would become new imperialism, with many changes - increased economic motive, different justifications for imperialism, and new/different states that became powerful.
New Imperialism gained an emphasis on raw materials for manufacturing. Now, imperialistic states had the capability to actually grow their own economies instead of relying on trade, and thus focused on acquiring the raw materials necessary to produce their products.
Furthermore, new imperialism relied on new justifications such as Social Darwinism, and focused more on civilizing rather than religious justifications.
New states also became powerful in the new imperialism era, especially Italy/Germany which would become new nations in this period. Furthermore, Spain and Portugal were not as powerful, with the British Empire being the strongest empire in this period.

Economic Imperialism

Economic imperialism is taking over a country using economics instead of directly controlling them militarily, which is much better for PR and also doesn’t cost as much. Many nations such as India become single-export economies and economically dependent on a single buyer, in India’s case cotton to the U.K leaving them economically dependent on a single material and weakening their economy (e.g if demand dropped, India's economy would be devastated).
This strategy was used heavily by European imperialistic nations for their ambitions, such as in India, where the British forced India to create cotton and opium.
However, sometimes it was supply side (force India to sell cotton), which made them economically dependent on British to buy cotton, or sometimes it was demand-side (China), where they became the chief supplier of some good like opium and controlling them through that method.
Remember - colonies didn't shift to become export economies, they were forced to become export/import economies, and economic imperialism is still imperialism with military control.

Economic Imperialism

It improved economic growth but large enterprises would replace skilled labor with mass unskilled labor and with have extremely low wages due to low regulations.
